About

I am a second-year PhD student in Computer Science at Virginia Tech, advised by Dr. Doug Bowman. I study how people organize information, make sense of complex data, and collaborate in immersive environments, with an interest in how AI can support these activities.

My work combines XR prototyping with controlled experiments and mixed-methods user studies. I design and evaluate spatial interaction techniques with the long-term goal of making immersive computing a practical alternative to the desktop.

Before starting my PhD in 2025, I pursued MEng studies at Virginia Tech and worked as a Software Engineer at Infosys. I hold a BTech in Information Technology from Guru Gobind Singh Indraprastha University.

IEEE International Symposium on Mixed and Augmented Reality (ISMAR) · 2025

Exploring Organizational Strategies in Immersive Computational Notebooks

S. In, A. Roy, E. Krokos, K. Whitley, C. North, Y. Yang

Abstract

Computational notebooks, which integrate code, documentation, tags, and visualizations into a single document, have become increasingly popular for data analysis tasks. With the advent of immersive technologies, these notebooks have evolved into a new paradigm, enabling more interactive and intuitive ways to perform data analysis. An immersive computational notebook, which integrates computational notebooks within an immersive environment, significantly enhances navigation performance with embodied interactions. However, despite recognizing the significance of organizational strategies in the immersive data science process, the organizational strategies for using immersive notebooks remain largely unexplored. In response, our research aims to deepen our understanding of organizations, especially focusing on spatial structures for computational notebooks, and to examine how various execution orders can be visualized in an immersive context. Through an exploratory user study, we found participants preferred organizing notebooks in half-cylindrical structures and engaged significantly more in non-linear analysis. Notably, as the scale of the notebooks increased (i.e., more code cells), users increasingly adopted multiple, concurrent non-linear analytical approaches.

Understanding Asynchronous Collaborative Sensemaking Through a Multi-Session Exploratory Study in Immersive Workspaces

A. Roy, C. Connor, S. In, K. Whitley, C. North, D. Bowman

▷Demo video coming soon
Draft abstract

Immersive workspaces allow analysts to externalize information and reasoning in persistent spatial arrangements, but how these workspaces support collaboration across time remains underexplored. We investigate asynchronous collaborative sensemaking through a multi-session exploratory study with 30 participants in 15 dyads. Partners alternated between individual sessions in a shared immersive workspace, inheriting and building on one another’s documents, notes, clusters, and links. We combined behavioral logs with semi-structured interviews to examine collaborative strategies, handoff practices, and workspace evolution. Our analysis identifies ways analysts preserve, extend, and selectively reorganize inherited work while balancing their own investigative goals with a partner’s ability to resume. Spatial groupings and authored notes offered entry points for continuation, yet incomplete explanations, unclear conventions, and accumulated visual complexity could leave incoming analysts reconstructing the reasoning behind an arrangement. These observations suggest that persistent spatial structure alone is insufficient to communicate analytical intent. We discuss implications for asynchronous immersive workspaces that make rationale, provenance, and changes legible while supporting both independent investigation and continuity between collaborators.

Understanding AI-Assisted Organization in Immersive Computational Notebook

S. In, J. Balayev, A. Roy, K. Whitley, C. North, M. Dasari

Abstract

Immersive computational notebooks allow analysts to organize data analysis in 3D space, supporting non-linear representations of complex analytical processes. However, as analyses grow in complexity, performing sensemaking remains effort-intensive, such as understanding analytical logic and making non-linear organizational decisions. To address this challenge, we explore the potential of AI-assisted organization for immersive computational notebooks, focusing on analysts’ sensemaking by having AI suggest non-linear organizational structures based on analytical logic and surrounding spatial context. To examine the impact of AI assistance on organizing immersive computational notebooks and understand how different levels of AI involvement affect the organizational process, we conducted a user study comparing three conditions: Manual, Suggestion, and Pre-defined. Our findings provide insights into how AI can support organizations in immersive computational notebooks while preserving human involvement.

Characterizing Cognitive Load in Immersive Spaces to Think

C. Connor, A. Roy, M. Baeck, S. In, K. Whitley, C. North, D. Bowman

Abstract

Sensemaking tasks place significant cognitive demands on analysts processing large volumes of information, yet the subjective cognitive load dynamics throughout these tasks remains poorly understood. We conducted an exploratory study examining cognitive load during immersive sensemaking using physiological indicators (heart rate, electrodermal activity, eyetracking) alongside subjective workload and interaction behaviors. While subjective workload increased over the task, no physiological modality improved population-level explanation of workload beyond time and individual differences. Skin conductance and pupil-workload relationships varied substantially across participants. Repeated note-taking was associated with flatter workload trajectories, suggesting a potential link between interaction strategies and cognitive demand management. These findings characterize how cognitive load unfolds during sustained sensemaking, and suggest that existing measurement approaches, often developed for short tasks where difficulty is artificially controlled, may not transfer to self-paced sensemaking work. Additionally, we present considerations for designing and evaluating cognitive load measurement in real-world, self-paced immersive tasks.

Evaluating Link Visualization Techniques for Sensemaking in Immersive Workspaces

A. Roy, I. Tahmid, F. Rodrigues, S. In, K. Whitley, C. North, D. Bowman

▷Demo video coming soon
Abstract

In immersive workspaces, sensemaking tasks often demand richer relational structure than spatial proximity alone can provide. Links make inter-document and inter-cluster relationships explicit, but as link density grows, the workspace quickly becomes cluttered and cognitively overwhelming. This motivates the need for link visualization techniques that reduce clutter without sacrificing relational interpretability. We evaluate how link routing and bundling techniques affect this tradeoff in a controlled 2 × 3 study across graph tidiness and comprehension tasks, using subjective measures and task performance. Results show that non-linear link routing improves perceived tidiness, while many-to-many bundling provides the most consistent benefits for tidiness, workload, usability, and completion time without reducing comprehension accuracy. Our findings indicate that both link routing and bundling affect how analysts manage visual complexity in immersive workspaces, improving efficiency and perceived clarity without compromising correctness. The optimal combination depends on task demands, balancing global overview readability with the need to trace specific connections efficiently.

Collaborative Spatial Negotiation in XR

Elastic Pose Negotiation · Ongoing project · IEEE VR 2027 3DUI Contest preparation

Draft abstract

Remote collaborators making spatial decisions must reconcile different object placements, orientations, and environmental constraints. Existing approaches to shared manipulation and collaborator awareness provide important foundations, but unresolved spatial intentions can remain difficult to inspect and negotiate. We present Elastic Pose Negotiation, an XR interaction technique that externalizes these intentions within a shared elastic structure containing editable replicas of complete object poses. Collaborators manipulate their own proposals while the structure continuously adapts to positional and rotational differences, making intermediate alternatives and disagreement visible without overwriting either proposal. Mutually feasible placement regions allow users to inspect concrete furniture replicas against their constraints before selecting a shared candidate. As proposals converge, the scaffold relaxes toward agreement; stretching, twisting, and fracture communicate increasing divergence and the need to reconfigure the negotiation. The rubber band serves as a shared negotiation scaffold connecting intentions, feasible alternatives, and emerging consensus. This ongoing project explores a unified direct-manipulation approach to translation, rotation, and constraint-aware spatial negotiation.

Research context

This project draws on prior work on collaborator-awareness cues, virtual replicas, and shared 3D object manipulation. Its focus is on making unresolved pose intentions and constraint-aware alternatives inspectable during negotiation.

Subtle Gaze Guidance in VR

Subtle Gaze Direction in Cinematic VR
Cherelle Connor, Yutong Ren, Ayush Roy

Abstract

Cinematic experiences use well-established editing techniques to guide user attention throughout narrative content. With the growing prominence of head-worn displays (HWD), there has been an increase in the use of this medium to create Cinematic Virtual Reality (CVR) experiences. However, given that CVR allows viewers to perceive content in 360 degrees, directors are no longer able to control how and when viewers see certain content within the scene. This provides an opportunity for bad actors to misguide users or misrepresent characters during the scene through the use of subtle gaze direction attacks. In this study, we investigate this threat by implementing subtle gaze direction cues within CVR scenes and examining whether they can redirect viewers’ attention away from relevant narrative content and toward attacker-selected content. We developed a subtle cue by modulating the luminance of a circular region of pixels within the scene. The cues were then placed either directly on a specific item or character within a region of interest (in-world cue) or in the periphery in order to guide the viewer to the appropriate region of interest (periphery cue). Our findings suggest that most viewers did not recognize the presence of the in-world cue and shifted their gaze toward the cue, showing a susceptibility to this type of attack. However, most of the participants did notice the periphery cue, but did not find it distracting. Overall, we believe that while the use of subtle gaze direction can provide unobtrusive guidance within CVR scenes, it also has the potential to redirect user attention and shape their perception of the content.

Multi-Agent Debiasing with Search-Based Validation

Ayush Roy, Mughees Ur Rehman · Fall 2025

Abstract

Large language models can reproduce social biases in their reasoning even when their final answers appear plausible. This project investigates whether iterative reasoning correction can reduce such bias while maintaining answer accuracy and contextual relevance. We developed a multi-agent framework in which a Judge agent evaluates a solver’s reasoning and a Rephrase agent revises it, with an optional search agent retrieving external evidence before revision. We evaluated the framework on subsets of BBQ-Hard and SQuAD v2 across four language models, examining bias, answer accuracy, and reasoning relevance. The experiments indicate that iterative rephrasing can reduce bias without consistently diminishing relevance to the question and context. However, reductions in reasoning-level bias did not uniformly translate into higher answer accuracy. Adding web search produced model-dependent effects, improving some outcomes while reducing performance in others. These findings highlight the need to evaluate fairness, grounding, and task performance jointly rather than treating external evidence as a universal improvement. The evaluation is limited by dataset subsets and a fixed number of revision iterations.
